The problem

02 The confidence trap

AI can be useful. That’s not the same as saying it is right.

Ask an AI tool a question about a market and you’ll usually get a confident answer. That is exactly why you need to be careful.

It can miss context, make things up, reinforce your assumptions, and produce a very convincing answer that is simply wrong. The answer isn’t to ignore it. It’s to use it differently.

The Sceptic’s Desk is built on one principle: AI should be your analyst, not your decision-maker.

03 The Sceptic’s Method

Five moves between an idea and an order.

Make AI do the work it is good at: structuring information, testing assumptions and helping you see what you missed.

  1. 01

    Research

    Gather information, analyse the market and build context before you form a view.

  2. 02

    Question

    Interrogate the first answer. Ask what is missing, assumed, or presented too confidently.

  3. 03

    Challenge

    Make AI argue the other side of your trade idea rather than reflect it back to you.

  4. 04

    Decide

    Apply your own judgement, define your risk, and keep the final decision human.

  5. 05

    Review

    Record what happened, then use the evidence to improve the process next time.

04 In practice

One trade idea, run through the whole method.

About twenty minutes, using four of the six tools in the Toolkit. Here is what that looks like from the inside.

Worked example · illustrative numbers

Nothing in that sequence told you what to trade.
Every step told you what you had not checked.

  1. 01

    The starting point

    A hunch, not yet a trade.

    £5,000 in the account and a strong stock that has pulled back to its 50-day average. Entry 228.50, stop 224.00, target 237.00. It feels right. Feeling right is not a reason.

  2. 02

    Research · Prompt Vault 09

    Establish what is actually driving it.

    You

    Here’s what happened to [instrument] over the last six weeks: [facts]. What are the plausible reasons, and how confident should I be in each? Don’t guess beyond the evidence.

    What comes back

    Three candidate drivers, ranked. Two rest on results published last month and are probably in the price. The third it flags as unverified.

  3. 03

    Challenge · Prompt Vault 15

    Make it argue against you.

    You

    Imagine it’s a year from now and this trade was a mistake. Write the post-mortem. What went wrong, in order of likelihood?

    What comes back

    Third pullback in six weeks, each shallower on lighter volume. A pattern more consistent with distribution than accumulation.

  4. 04

    Size · Position Size Calculator

    Let arithmetic decide the size.

    Position size
    11.11
    Cash at risk
    £50.00
    Reward-to-risk
    1.89:1
  5. 05

    Check · Trade Checklist

    Then a box refuses to tick.

    Pre-trade gates

    • Setup matches a written rule · yes
    • Risk is 1% or less · £50 of £5,000
    • No scheduled event within three days · clear
    • Reward-to-risk meets my 2:1 minimum · 1.89:1 — fails
  6. 06

    Record · The Sceptic’s Journal

    Log the trade you did not take.

    Instrument, reason, how you felt, whether you followed the plan. Twenty of those and you can finally see whether your discipline is real or theoretical.
