So far, AI has argued against your trade, surfaced its assumptions, and listed what already contradicts it. This week, the fourth of the five questions:

What am I potentially overlooking?

It can sound like a repeat of the last two. It isn't. Assumptions sit inside your reasoning. Contradicting evidence sits inside the material you gathered. This question is about everything outside both: the things that never made it onto your screen at all.

"Is there anything I've missed?"

Ask it like that and the AI only has what you gave it. It reviews your reasoning, finds no gaps in it, and says so. The gaps were never in the reasoning. They were in what you left out before you started.

The frame is the blind spot

Take a hypothetical long on a commodity future after it breaks above a range it has held for several weeks. You've studied the chart, the range, and the volume on the break. The analysis is careful, and it is all about the chart.

What isn't on the chart: a scheduled data release two days into your intended holding period. An existing position elsewhere in your account that carries much the same exposure. The cost of holding the contract for longer than you planned, or a roll date that falls inside your timeframe. A weekly chart that tells a different story from the daily. The fact that you'll be away from your screen on Thursday.

None of that is hidden. You simply didn't look, because you were looking at the thing you'd decided mattered.

A prompt that does the job

"Here is my trade idea: [instrument, direction, entry, stop, target, and timeframe]. My analysis covered: [what you actually looked at]. My other open positions are: [list, or none]. Work through what I may be overlooking under these headings: scheduled events in my timeframe, overlap with my other positions, costs and contract mechanics, other timeframes, and practical constraints on managing the trade. For each heading, tell me what I should check and why it matters for this setup. Don't state current dates or figures as fact; tell me what to verify and where."

Three things make it work:

  1. It tells the AI what you did look at, so it can point to what you didn't.
  2. It supplies the headings. Without them, an AI coach drifts back to generic market risk, which is the one area you have already covered.
  3. It asks for things to check, not facts. An AI coach may not have today's calendar or your broker's terms, and a confident wrong date is worse than none.

What to do with the answer

Treat the output as a to-do list and work through it before you enter. For each item, one of three things will be true:

  1. Checked, and it doesn't matter. Note it in a line and move on. You'll be glad of the line when you review the trade.
  2. Checked, and it changes the plan. Adjust before entry, not after. That might mean a shorter holding period, a smaller size because two positions are really one, or a different entry day.
  3. Can't check it. Then you are carrying a risk you can't see. Say so in your plan, and let it count against your size.

Keep the headings. After a few weeks you'll find the same gaps turning up, and they are worth adding to your own pre-trade checklist so you stop needing the prompt for them.

You can't see everything. You can make a habit of asking where you haven't looked.
